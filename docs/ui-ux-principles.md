# UI/UX and layout principles

This is the canonical guidance for user-facing interfaces when introduced in
cognix. It defines general principles without selecting features, a layout or a
framework.

1. **Keep it simple.** Show necessary information and actions. Add controls,
   panels or steps only when they serve a concrete user need.
2. **Show, don't tell.** Communicate structure through spacing, alignment,
   grouping and visual hierarchy. Avoid redundant headings, repeated helper
   prose, decorative status messages and unnecessary panels.
3. **Make interaction intuitive and predictable.** Use familiar patterns,
   consistent appearance and behavior, and feedback that supports the task.

## Task hierarchy and discoverability

Prioritize the user's main task, content and result. Group controls near their
effects and related controls together. Choose layouts from actual tasks and
available space, considering obstruction, switching cost and access to content.

Keep everyday actions discoverable. Expose advanced or contextual controls when
relevant, without hiding necessary ordinary actions just to reduce visual density.

## Responsive layout and accessibility

Maintain readable content and responsive layouts across available screen sizes;
keep necessary actions reachable when space is limited. Support keyboard access,
logical focus order, visible focus, accessible names and meaningful labels.
Do not rely solely on hover, color or other visual cues to communicate actions or
state. Preserve essential instructions even when simplifying the presentation.

## Feedback and safeguards

Provide useful progress feedback, actionable errors and clear recovery paths
when needed. Avoid messages that merely decorate an already clear state. Preserve
necessary consent, warnings and destructive-action safeguards so simplification
does not remove information needed to act safely.
